How much does a website cost?

There is no single price: a three-page site and an online shop have nothing in common. Here is what really drives the price, and how to read a quote without bad surprises.

Updated on 22 September 2026 · 2 min read

In short

  • The price depends mostly on what the site has to do, not on how many pages it has.
  • Also plan for yearly costs: domain name, hosting, maintenance.
  • A good quote states what is included, what isn't, and who owns the site.

What drives the price

Five things really weigh on a quote. The first is what the site has to do: presenting a business costs far less than selling online or taking bookings.

Then come the number of pages, the number of languages, the content to produce (copy, photos, video) and the timeline requested.

  • Features: showcase, bookings, online sales, customer area
  • Number of pages and languages
  • Content: supplied by you, or to be produced
  • Timeline: a faster pace takes more time

Yearly costs

A website doesn't only cost money on the day it's built. Every year the domain name must be renewed and hosting paid, usually a few tens of euros a year for a showcase site.

Maintenance may come on top: updates, backups, small changes. It can be monthly or on request.

Traps to avoid

Be wary of very cheap offers with an endless monthly subscription: after a few years you've often paid more, and the site isn't yours.

Also check that the domain name is registered in your name: it's your address on the internet and should stay yours.

  • A site that disappears if you stop the subscription
  • A domain name registered to the provider
  • A quote that doesn't say what's included

How to compare two quotes

Compare what is actually delivered: number of pages, features, SEO, languages, training to manage the site. A cheaper quote that leaves out SEO will cost more in lost customers.

Finally, ask who owns the site once it's paid for, and what happens if you change provider.
